Aim: To measure the frequency, wavelength and speed of waves in a ripple tank and on a string.

Open: physicsgcse.prodigyai.uk → Practical Lab → Waves

Before you start

1

Write the wave equation. Give the unit of each quantity.

2

Why is it better to measure across ten waves and divide by ten, than to measure one wave?

Method: Part A — ripple tank

  1. 1
    Choose Ripple tank. Set the frequency to 6 Hz.
  2. 2
    Press Freeze the pattern. Drag the two orange markers onto bright lines, as far apart as you can. Count the whole waves between them and press Record.
    Each gap between two bright lines is one wave.
  3. 3
    Copy the distance and the number of waves into Table 1. Calculate λ = distance ÷ waves, then v = f × λ.
  4. 4
    Repeat for the other frequencies in Table 1.

Method: Part B — waves on a string

  1. 5
    Choose Waves on a string and a hanging mass of 200 g. The vibrating length is 1.00 m.
  2. 6
    Change the frequency until you see a clear standing wave, then press Record. Count the loops.
    Each loop is half a wavelength.
  3. 7
    Find three different patterns (different numbers of loops). Calculate λ = 2 × 1.00 m ÷ number of loops, then v = f × λ.
